<Before>
Unfired clay is useful and temporary. It can be shaped wet and dried hard in the sun, and it will
hold that shape until it meets water again, at which point it slumps back into mud. Storage is the
casualty. Grain and oil keep only as well as the container keeps out damp and vermin, and a basket
or a skin does neither for long. A society that grows more food than it eats within the season
needs a vessel that does not dissolve.
</Before>

<Breakthrough>
Fired vessels appear at very different dates in different places — pots from Xianrendong in China
are around eighteen thousand years old — but pottery becomes a general technology of settled life
in the Near East by roughly 7000 BCE, alongside grain agriculture. The method is to hold a shaped
pot in a fire hot enough, and for long enough, that the clay changes chemically rather than merely
drying. The first firings are open bonfires reaching perhaps 700°; enclosing the fire in a kiln
with a draught raises that to 900° or more and makes the result consistent.
</Breakthrough>

<Science numbers="550° dehydroxylation · 900° firing · irreversible">
Clay is a stack of hydrated aluminosilicate platelets, and water sits in it in two ways. Free water
between the platelets lets the clay slide and be shaped, and evaporates below 100°. Hydroxyl groups
built into the crystal structure itself do not. Above about 550° those come out as steam in a
reaction called dehydroxylation, and what is left is a different mineral: it cannot take the water
back, so it cannot become plastic again. Push on to 900° and the particles begin to sinter, fusing
at their contact points into a rigid ceramic body.

That single threshold is why the entry sits on this road. Everything before it is shaping; this is
the first case of heat used to change what a material is rather than what shape it holds.

<Forward year="7000 BCE">
A grain jar is the least dramatic object on this road and one of the most consequential: it is how
a harvest survives to the following spring, which is how a settlement survives a bad year. But the
kiln matters more than the pot. Once people are building enclosures that hold a chosen temperature
and force a draught through fuel, the same structure can be aimed at ore instead of clay
<Modern>(<Ref to="the-bloomery-furnace">the bloomery furnace</Ref>, P.II)</Modern>. Every entry on
the materials road for the next eight thousand years is this argument at a higher number, until

</Forward>

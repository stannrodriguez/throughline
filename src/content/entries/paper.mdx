<Before>
Every writing surface before this is either scarce or awkward. Clay is heavy and cannot be bound
into a volume. Papyrus grows in one river valley and rots outside a dry climate. Parchment is
excellent — durable, foldable, takes ink cleanly — and it is made from the skin of an animal that
had to be raised, killed and processed. A full Bible runs to the skins of a couple of hundred
sheep. At that price, writing is reserved for things worth the herd: scripture, law, contracts,
accounts. Nobody drafts.
</Before>

<Breakthrough>
The Chinese court official Cai Lun is credited in 105 CE with a process that had probably been
developing for some time: beat rags, hemp waste, bark and old fishing net in water until the fibres
separate, dip a screen into the vat, lift it so the water drains through and a mat of tangled fibre
is left on the mesh, then press and dry the sheet. Paper stays a Chinese technology for six
centuries. It reaches Samarkand in the eighth century, Baghdad by 794 where the first paper mill
outside China is built, and Islamic Spain by the tenth.
</Breakthrough>

<Science numbers="~200 skins per parchment Bible · 1 vat, many sheets · 105 CE">
Paper is a nonwoven sheet, and that is the whole economy of it. A woven cloth needs threads spun
and then ordered on a loom. Paper needs no order at all: cellulose fibres beaten in water fray into
fine hairs, and when the water drains they mat into a dense random tangle held together by hydrogen
bonding between the cellulose surfaces. No glue, no thread, no animal. Sizing with starch or gelatin
stops ink from feathering, and beating the pulp longer gives a smoother, stronger sheet.

The raw material is the point. Rag and plant waste are things a city already has and would
otherwise throw away, so supply scales with population rather than with livestock. Paper is
genuinely worse than parchment in durability, and it wins on volume, which is the same trade the
materials road makes over and over.

<Forward year="105">
A cheap sheet changes what is worth writing down. Notes, drafts, letters, ledgers, school
exercises, complaints — categories of document that were never worth a sheepskin — become routine,
and the survival rate of ordinary life in the record goes up sharply. The greater consequence
waits three centuries beyond this period. A press capable of three hundred impressions an hour
implies a supply of sheets by the thousand, which no flock can meet; the printing revolution
happens where the paper mills already are <Modern>(<Ref to="movable-type">movable type</Ref>,
P.V)</Modern>.
</Forward>

<Before>
A body that eats only raw food spends a great deal of its day getting the food into a usable state.
Chimpanzees chew for something close to half their waking hours; the human figure is around five
percent. Raw meat resists tearing, raw tubers are largely indigestible starch locked inside cell
walls, and both carry parasites. Night is unusable: no work after dark, and predators have the
advantage. Cold sets the edge of the habitable range.
</Before>

<Breakthrough>
The change is not making fire but keeping it. Hearths appear in the same spots in the same caves
across long stretches of time, which means fire is being fed, banked overnight and carried between
sites rather than struck fresh each evening. Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa holds burnt bone and
ash deep inside the cave, away from any lightning strike. By roughly 400,000 years ago the evidence
at sites such as Qesem Cave is of habitual use: the same hearth, in the same place, over many
generations.
</Breakthrough>

<Science numbers="5% of the day chewing · 48% for a chimpanzee · 550°">
Cooking does two chemical things. Heat above about 60° gelatinises starch, splitting the tight
crystalline granules in a tuber or a grain so that digestive enzymes can reach them; and it
denatures protein, unfolding the coiled molecules in meat so they come apart under an enzyme
instead of resisting it. The result is not more calories in the food but more of them reaching the
bloodstream, at a lower cost in chewing and gut work. Heat also kills the parasites that raw meat
carries.

The other half of the entry is that fire is a tool that makes tools. A wooden spear tip hardened in
flame holds a point. Ash and heat treatment change how flint fractures, giving a cleaner edge.
Above about 550°, the same heat begins to drive chemically bound water out of clay, which is the
threshold every later furnace is a refinement of.

<Forward year="400,000 BCE">
The hearth is the only entry on this road that nobody invented and no date will fix. What it hands
forward is a threshold rather than a device: once a fire can be held at a chosen temperature for a
chosen time, everything that follows is a matter of enclosing it better. Put a roof and a draught
on it and clay becomes permanent <Modern>(<Ref to="fired-pottery">fired pottery</Ref>, same
period)</Modern>. Force air through it and rock gives up metal <Modern>(<Ref
to="the-bloomery-furnace">the bloomery furnace</Ref>, P.II)</Modern>. The entire materials road is
a record of people getting a fire hotter, and it starts here.
</Forward>
